Featured self-initiated case study

Travel Together
The Agent Growth League

A proposed four-week community-led marketing pilot designed to help travel agents learn from one another, discover useful promotional content, and create more qualified opportunities for travel bookings.

Community engagementReferral strategyBusiness growthMeasurement & ROI

01 / The opportunity

Community activity is more valuable when members have a reason to return and can connect participation to a practical business need. This concept focuses on travel agents—not just consumers—so community engagement and business activity can support each other.

Who it is for

  • Active agents looking for fresh selling ideas.
  • Registered but inactive agents who may need a reason to re-engage.
  • New agents who could benefit from beginner-friendly guidance.

02 / Campaign mechanics

  • “Can You Sell This?” — a weekly destination challenge where agents share a suitable customer pitch or itinerary angle.
  • Travel Buddy referrals — invite relevant agents, with recognition tied to verified activation, not just signup.
  • Ready-to-share kits — approved destination creatives, sample captions, FAQs, and promotional prompts.
  • Agent Spotlight + Growth Room — recognise useful contributions and host short practical learning sessions.

03 / Four-week rollout

WEEK 1
Invite & activateSegment agents, launch the challenge, and record baseline activity.
WEEK 2
Build participationRelease marketing kits, run a learning session, and feature an agent.
WEEK 3
Connect to businessHighlight suitable products and track qualified interest and bookings.
WEEK 4
Review & improveRecognise members, gather feedback, and evaluate campaign economics.

04 / Illustrative pilot budget

A capped budget encourages the team to test the idea before scaling it.

Digital creatives & templates₹2,000
Online session & coordination₹1,000
Limited rewards₹5,000
Capped referral incentives₹4,000
Contingency₹3,000
Total pilot budget₹15,000

Cost control: use existing channels and internal resources, avoid blanket discounts, cap rewards, and count only valid qualifying activity.

05 / How success would be measured

AttractionInvitations, sign-ups, referral conversion
EngagementActive members, submissions, repeat participation
BusinessQualified enquiries, incremental qualifying bookings
EfficiencyCost per activated agent, contribution, net result

The team could test whether adding an interactive challenge to a destination promotion improves engagement or qualified enquiries compared with a standard promotion. Targets should be set using real baseline data rather than assumed success percentages.

06 / Break-even logic

Net campaign result = (incremental qualifying bookings × contribution per booking) − campaign cost.

For illustration only, if the contribution were ₹1,000 per incremental qualifying booking and the pilot cost were ₹15,000, the campaign would need 15 incremental qualifying bookings to break even. Actual margins and booking attribution would need to be confirmed with the business team.

To protect profitability, rewards would be capped, blanket discounts avoided, cancellations considered, and incremental bookings distinguished from bookings that would have happened anyway.

This is an original, self-initiated strategy proposal created to demonstrate marketing thinking. It is not an executed campaign, has no claimed results, and is not presented as an official TravClan initiative. The budget and unit economics are illustrative assumptions, not company data.
